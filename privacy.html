Privacy statement

Privacy statement: Your details,
kept to a minimum.

What happens to the information you send through this website, in plain words.

Who is responsible

This website belongs to CASA, run by Raffaella Amista in Schiedam, the Netherlands. She is responsible for the personal data described here. For any question about it, write to info@casa-schiedam.nl.

No cookies, no tracking

This website does not use cookies, analytics, advertising pixels or embedded videos and maps. Fonts and images are served from this site itself, so visiting a page doesn't share anything with Google or social networks.

The site is hosted on GitHub Pages (GitHub, Inc.). Like any web server, it briefly logs technical data such as your IP address to deliver the pages and keep them secure. Raffaella has no access to those logs.

The contact form

When you send the contact form, Raffaella receives what you typed: your name, email address, and — if you fill them in — your phone number, company, preferred date, number of guests, location, budget and your message. Nothing is sent until you press Send.

She uses these details only to answer your request and, if you go ahead, to prepare and carry out the catering, workshop or event you asked about. The legal basis is taking the steps you asked for before (and while) making an agreement with you. Your details are never sold, shared for marketing, or added to a mailing list.

The form is delivered by Web3Forms, which passes the message on as an email. Email is handled by Proton Mail (Proton AG, Switzerland). Both act only as service providers for delivering and storing that message.

How long it's kept

Messages that don't lead to a booking are deleted within two years of the last contact. If you do book, the details needed for invoicing are kept for seven years, because Dutch tax law requires it.

Your rights

You can ask to see the data Raffaella holds about you, to correct it, to have it deleted, or to receive a copy. Email info@casa-schiedam.nl and you'll get an answer within a month.

If you think your data has been handled wrongly, you can also complain to the Dutch data protection authority, the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens.
