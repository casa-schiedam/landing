About Raffaella Amista

About Raffaella Amista: A Neapolitan kitchen,
then the Netherlands,
then an idea.

Story, experience, and what she's building next — from a childhood kitchen in Naples to Momo Cooking, Melting Pot, and now CASA.

Story

The same path,
different tools.

Hi, I’m Raffaella, and I’m from Naples.

It’s the first thing I like to say about myself. Not simply because it’s the place where I was born and grew up, but because Naples is the context that shaped me and that, in some way, is still present in every aspect of my life: socially, professionally, and emotionally.

I was born in the 1980s (I won’t tell you exactly when, you can try to guess) and I studied architecture. After graduating, I spent several years working in social and cultural research, working with communities and in close contact with people.

And what does all of this have to do with architecture? Much more than you might think.

What has always interested me is not only buildings, but the people who inhabit them, the spaces where they meet, the relationships they build, and the way a community takes shape. In the end, I’ve continued to explore the same things, simply from different perspectives: people and the spaces they create to be together.

And then there is food.

My passion for cooking didn’t begin at any particular moment. It has always been part of my life. Since I was a child, when my mother opened up her kitchen to me and taught me everything she knew.

For me, cooking is an act of care. It is a way of transforming and passing on knowledge. It is not just about nourishment: it is knowledge itself. It is memory, experience, and storytelling. It is the way something we have received from someone else can be transformed and then passed on again.

It is also a way of telling a story. And at some point, I decided I wanted to tell mine.

When I arrived in the Netherlands, I found myself in a new country, with a different language, culture, and way of life. And, as happens when you arrive somewhere where nobody knows you, there was one very simple thing I had to do: introduce myself.

To say who I am. Where I come from. What I bring with me. And perhaps, too, what I want to become.

My cooking became my way of doing that.

Through what I cook, I started to tell the story of Naples, my family, what I had learned, the people who had taught me something, and the places I come from. But also the story of my journey and of the person I have become along the way.

I realised that food could be my language. A language capable of going beyond a language I did not yet speak well, and of creating a shared space between people who might otherwise have had nothing in common.

And this is where all the different paths I had taken began to meet: architecture, my interest in communities, cooking, care, and the need to create spaces where people can come together.

Perhaps that is why, today, what I want to build is not simply a place to eat.

I want to create a space where food can continue to be what it has always been for me: a way of caring for one another, sharing knowledge, telling a story, and, above all, being together.

Experience

Five years of kitchens,
tables and people.

For the past five years, I’ve been building Momo’ Cooking through home catering, workshops, private dinners, celebrations and events, across Delft, The Hague and Rotterdam.

Cooking has always been only part of it. Through the workshops, I’ve had the chance to share what I know — recipes, techniques, stories and values — and to see how food can become a way of learning, connecting and creating something together.

Along the way, I’ve had the privilege of collaborating with many wonderful people and being part of very different projects. I led a fantastic team for an Eetcafe in Delft, working with and for people in vulnerable situations, and cooked for special events supporting a community canteen in Rotterdam.

One of the most meaningful collaborations came from meeting another Neapolitan, Valentina. Together, we created Melting Pot, a workshop format built around food and guided conversations on different topics. We cook together, but the food is only part of the experience: it becomes a way to open up, listen, share and sometimes simply feel a little less alone.

And somewhere along the way, I also became a mother to a wonderful little girl, Daphne.

Five years, hundreds of people, countless meals, collaborations and stories later, I’ve realised that what I want to do with food is much more than simply cook.

I want to share knowledge, create connections and bring people together around a table.

And after five years of creating these moments wherever I could, I’ve started to imagine what could happen if, instead of moving from one place to another, I had a place of my own…

Ambition

CASA —
a place to be.

For the past five years, I’ve seen how much can happen around a table when people have a place to meet, cook, share and simply be together.

Now I want to give those moments a home.

CASA is my vision for a permanent space in Schiedam: a place with its own kitchen and a table at its heart, where Momo Cooking can grow into something more, a place for workshops, private dinners, a small everyday bistro, collaborations and new ideas.

A place where food is not only prepared and served, but where knowledge is shared, stories are exchanged and people can feel part of something.

After five years of building Momo one event at a time, I’m ready to build the space that brings all these pieces together.

CASA is that next step.

Momo Cooking

Where it started.

Momo Cooking was born in Delft in 2021, during Covid, as a home-catering project offering weekly menus. At a time when so much was missing: closeness, connection, the simple act of sharing a meal. I wanted to create food that could bring a little of that back.

What started with weekly menus grew into small and medium-sized caterings, private dinners, workshops and countless encounters around food.

Today, Momo Cooking is still rooted in the same idea: hands-on Italian cooking, shared knowledge and food made to bring people together.

Melting Pot

Where cooking becomes conversation.

Melting Pot was born from my encounter with Valentina, another Neapolitan who shares my belief in the power of food to bring people together.

Together, we created a workshop format that combines cooking with guided conversation. While I bring the hands-on experience of making and sharing food, Valentina guides the conversation, creating a space where people can open up, listen to one another and explore a different topic each time.

The act of cooking, working with our hands and creating something together becomes the starting point for a deeper kind of exchange. Food gives us something to do together, while conversation gives us a space to share, reflect and connect.

The result is something more than a cooking workshop: a special, sometimes therapeutic way of being together.

Melting Pot currently has a regular appointment at Molen De Roos in Delft and is also available on request for other locations, private groups and events.
